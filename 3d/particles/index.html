Particles

pixi-solid-3d exposes both particle classes from @pixi/3d, and they do different jobs.

<ParticleSystem3D><ParticleContainer3D>
Simulationbuilt innone
You writenothing per frameevery slot, every frame
Good forfire, smoke, sparks, rain, dustparticles a physics or gameplay system owns
Import@pixi/3d/extras@pixi/3d

ParticleSystem3D extends ParticleContainer3D, so it is the container plus an emitter. If you are not computing particle positions yourself, use <ParticleSystem3D> and skip the rest of the container section.

Simulated particles

<ParticleSystem3D> emits particles from a shape, moves them under gravity and drag, and animates their size, colour and opacity over their life. Everything is driven by props.

Why a component?

In @pixi/3d, ParticleSystem3D steps itself from Pixi's global Ticker.shared. A defaultApplication runs that separately from app.ticker, so a new particle can show a frame late.

<ParticleSystem3D>:

  1. Follows your ticker: steps from the nearest contextual ticker (from <PixiCanvas>,<PixiApplicationProvider> or <TickerProvider>) instead of a global timer.

  2. Updates live: emitRate, gravity, drag, playing and frameRate are reactive, so a signal changes the running emitter without rebuilding its GPU buffers.

  3. Cleans up: on unmount it frees the buffers and the ticker callback.

Emitter shapes

Shapes come from @pixi/3d/extras:

  • ConeShape({ angle, radius }) spawns on an XZ disc and shoots upward in a cone. Fire plumes, exhaust, fountains, torches.

  • SphereShape({ radius, surfaceOnly }) spawns inside or on a sphere, radiating outward. Magic orbs, explosions, bursts.

  • BoxShape({ width, height, depth }) spawns across a volume. Ambient dust, snow, rain.

  • PointShape() spawns everything from one origin.

Spreads: ranges versus fixed numbers

speed, life, size, sizeEnd, alpha, alphaEnd and startRotation accept spreads:

  • Fixed scalar: speed={4} gives every particle speed 4.

  • Range: speed={[2, 5]} randomises between 2 and 5 per particle.

Additive versus normal blending

  • blendMode="add" brightens what is behind: fire, glowing sparks, laser exhaust, magic.

  • blendMode="normal" composites normally: smoke, dust, leaves, debris.

Hand-driven particles

<ParticleContainer3D> is a pool of quads and nothing more. It never moves a particle, so your frame loop writes every slot and then calls upload() once.

Writing slots

Three ways, all of which allocate nothing per particle:

Call upload() once, after all writes for the frame. Nothing appears until you do. A slot with size0 draws nothing, and clear() empties the pool.

setParticle(index, values) also exists but takes an object literal, so reserve it for spawning.

Letting the GPU animate

Writing every particle every frame is only necessary when you own the simulation. When the motion is predictable, extraBits moves the work into the vertex shader: a bit declares per-particle attributes and uniforms, so you write each particle once and a frame only advances a uniform.

Then the frame loop is one line:

An unknown attribute throws at spawn time and lists the attributes the bits declare.

<ParticleSystem3D> derives the bits it needs from its own props, so you never name one there.extraBits on a system only adds a bit you wrote yourself, which is an advanced case.

See the @pixi/3d particle bits guide for writing your own.
